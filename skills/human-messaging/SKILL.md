# Human Messaging — Write Like a Person, Not a Sequence

Read `SELLER-CONFIG.md` (voice samples, personas, diagnostic questions) before drafting. For the full research base, read this skill's `references/`: Human_Selling_Framework_Library.md (Gap Selling, Sandler, PULL, tactical empathy, Josh Braun - the synthesis), Human_Cordiality_Repo.md (warmth and rapport, tiered and sourced), and Sample_Messaging_Library.md (one worked example email per scenario).

## The checklist every draft must pass

1. **Why them, why now, why you** - tied to something TRUE: their role, their company's actual situation, a real change on your side (new pricing, new capability, you took over the territory).
2. **Ask, don't assert, their pain** (Gap Selling). You do not know their problems. One diagnostic question they can answer in a sentence.
3. **One question per email.** Two questions = zero answers.
4. **Explicit room for no** (Sandler). "If this isn't a priority, tell me and I'll leave you alone" energy, without saying it robotically. The decline-and-it-disappears line does this in covers.
5. **Label, never narrate** their behavior. Never "I noticed you opened my email" or "you didn't respond to my last note." Reference the touchpoint without asserting an outcome.
6. **Give or ask, never just re-ask.** A follow-up that reworders the same ask is spam. Teach something, share something relevant, or ask a genuinely different question.
7. **Read it aloud.** If it sounds like a sales email, rewrite it.

## Structure of a cover email

```
Hi {First},

{1-2 sentences: the true reason for reaching out - their company + role + what changed on your side.}

From the {persona} seat, could I get 20-30 minutes for a {health check / gut check} on {the thing they own}? {One diagnostic question from the config.}

I can send a hold for {Day, Month Date} at {time in THEIR timezone}. Decline and it disappears.

Best,
{full signature from config, name bold}
```

## Banned, always

- Em dashes. Use a period, comma, or colon.
- "Just circling back", "just following up", "touch base", "checking in", "per my last email"
- Manufactured urgency ("spots filling fast")
- Self-disclaimers ("not a generic pitch", "not trying to sell you", "no pressure")
- "Hope this finds you well"
- Anything narrating their behavior back at them
- Guilt framing in follow-ups

## Voice rules

- Greeting: "Hi {First}," then a blank line. Sign-off: "Best,"
- Short sentences. No jargon. A stranger should understand every line.
- HTML body with the signature name in bold. Check one draft per batch renders correctly.
- Subject lines that say what it is: "{Their Company} x {Your Company}", "A question for {Company}'s {role}". No clickbait.

## Threading

Before drafting any message to someone you have emailed before: search ALL mail (including trash) for the existing thread and reply within it. New threads to known contacts are a bug.
