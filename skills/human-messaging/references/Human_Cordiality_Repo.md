# Human Cordiality Repo
How humans win humans over in conversation. Companion to Human_Selling_Framework_Library.md (the sales-methodology layer). This file is the warmth/rapport layer plus internet-native evidence. Compiled 2026-07-21 from a 5-stream deep research pass (Reddit via archives/aggregators, creator content, internet culture) plus the book/research canon. ## Tier 1: Strongly corroborated (multiple independent communities/sources)

### 1. Attention allocation is the whole game
Total undivided attention, names at hello AND goodbye, remembering small details, being visibly glad to see people. The recurring Clinton anecdote: "he made you feel like the most important person in the room" and knew your name a half hour later. Fred Rogers introduced himself to the bicycle messenger.
- Outreach application: reply to what THEY said before pivoting; reference details from prior touches; greet warmly on reconnect.
- Sources: r/AskReddit thread 1pkyvmk (via boredpanda.com/social-situations-psychological-cheat-codes), andreapetkovic.substack.com/p/what-is-charisma, paulgraham.com/recharisma.html

### 2. Compliment people behind their backs
Praising absent people signals you build others up when they can't hear it; trust transfers to you. Top-voted AskReddit "cheat code" (2 independent top comments + external corroboration).
- Outreach application: praise a prospect's colleague/team's public work; speak well of competitors' people; assume forwarding.

### 3. Demand nothing (the most-shared charisma essay)
"The most charismatic people actively invite you to things with your best interests in mind but are totally okay with you declining, and they really mean it." No guilt, no strings, no neediness.
- Outreach application: "decline and it disappears" IS this principle. The out must be genuine; one empathy reply max after a decline, then respect the answer.
- Source: altered.substack.com/p/charisma (15k likes)

### 4. Calibrated effort (internet culture's unified theory)
Maximum effort understanding the other person; minimum visible effort on self-presentation. "Rizz" = charisma with no visible machinery; visible technique flips it to cringe. Cringe = visible strain + misread audience. Forced casualness is also cringe; sincere specific enthusiasm is the new cool. Effort spent on THEM is charming; effort spent on being impressive is cringe.
- Outreach application: no visible frameworks, no pattern-interrupt gimmicks, no breathless follow-ups, no manufactured cool. Write like someone who doesn't need the meeting.
- Sources: knowyourmeme.com rizz guide, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizz, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cringe_culture, rollingstone.com cringe-creators piece

### 5. Lurk more (Rule 33 of the Internet)
Goodwill is earned by observing before speaking. Longer lurkers accumulate cultural capital; premature posters get roasted. Prove you lurked: reference something only an observer would know; never ask what their website answers.
- Outreach application: the pre-vet/research protocol is this rule. Every cover proves research (their stack, their titles, their season).
- Sources: globaltill.com/rule-33-lurk-more-internet-rules, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurker

### 6. Don't main-character
The prospect is the main character of every email. Cut I/we openers, company bragging, award mentions. Self-aware admissions ("you probably get twenty of these a week") earn goodwill by showing you know how you look from their side.
- Sources: psychologytoday.com main-character-syndrome, Cleveland Clinic

### 7. Yes-and the bit
When someone offers humor, a sports reference, or a playful premise, build on it before returning to business. Killing a bit with a canned pivot is a social foul. A prospect who jokes is offering a relationship premise.
- Source: bcheights.com/210973 commit-to-the-bit; improv canon

### 8. Parasocial warmth (the streamer playbook)
People bond with whoever makes them feel individually seen: acknowledgment by name, responding to what they said, consistent predictable cadence (not erratic bursts), celebrating their wins with no ask attached. Warm but boundaried.
- Sources: blerp.com streamers parasocial guide, ResearchGate Twitch sense-of-community study

### 9. r/coldemail tracked-data rules
- Micro-yes CTA beats meeting ask: "Want me to send it?" moved 0.8% to 3-4% replies (tracked 200+ replies).
- Under 80 words, plain text, one link max. 6-8 sentences = 6.9% reply (Belkins 16.5M-email study).
- Pronoun ratio at least 2:1 you/your over I/my/we.
- Trigger-event openers (job change <30d, funding, launch) = 4x replies; decays after ~60 days.
- Do a piece of work first: annotated-teardown campaign hit 34% replies, ~60% of video viewers booked.
- Follow-ups: 42% of replies come from them; every touch adds something new; pressure-free breakups often out-reply every other touch.
- Humility openers ("Am I reaching the right person?") get forwarded with implicit endorsement.
- 1-2 contacts per company = 7.8% reply; 10+ = 3.8%, spam complaints triple by email 4.
- Loss-frame the specific leak beats gain-frame ("you're probably losing X at checkout" > "we can grow conversions").
- Benchmarks: 3.43% avg reply, 5.5% top quartile, 10.7%+ elite (Instantly 2026).
- Sources: reddit.com/r/coldemail/comments/1pd450r (via prospeo.io/s/cold-email-examples), commissionbreath.substack.com/p/hi-im-pat-want-to-get-married

## Tier 2: Single-source but high-value
- Ben Franklin effect: tiny favors build liking ("their brain justifies helping by deciding they like you").
- "Are you venting, asking for advice, or asking me to fix it?" — give people a defined role before responding.
- Sam Elliott pulling weeds with the plumber: charm as joining someone's mundane reality instead of holding court.
- Warm exit: enthusiasm + stated mission + early exit beats lingering.
- Name-recovery story: if they forgot your name, tell a quick story where someone says it.
- Admit mistakes instantly ("disarms everybody") — validated by the Raycom win, see Observed Wins.
- Instantly calming presence: "the most regulated nervous system in the room" (LeVar Burton anecdotes).

## Tier 3: Creator techniques (endorsement vs backlash noted)
- Charisma on Command: three conversation modes (news/fun/values — skeptics thaw in fun first); reveal a weakness early; name the tension ("I get the sense you've heard this pitch before"). BACKLASH: formulaic celebrity-breakdown tricks read as performed.
- Vinh Giang (least backlash, most endorsed): conversational threading (answers with 3-4 hooks); cold-reading positive guesses as questions; vocal mirror then lift; strategic pause; downward inflection; "yes, and" over "yes, but".
- Jefferson Fisher (most uniformly endorsed): never win an argument, make it safe for the NEXT conversation; "I see things differently"; "Maybe you're right" (tone-sensitive); conversational contract opener ("My goal is for us to get on the same page. Does that sound good?"); silence can never be misquoted.
- Vanessa Van Edwards (most polarized): warmth before competence for skeptics; verbal engagement cues; "I was just thinking of you" (signal amplification bias: people underestimate that you like them). BACKLASH: checklist body-language cues look performed.

## Book/research canon (the layer under all of this)
Carnegie (genuine interest, names, let others talk); Cialdini (liking, unity); FBI Behavioral Change Stairway (listen > empathize > rapport > influence, no skipping — the cause-before-mechanics rule); Duhigg's Supercommunicators (match conversation type: practical/emotional/social); Brooks' How to Know a Person (illuminators vs diminishers); Alison Wood Brooks (follow-up questions are the biggest likability driver); Epley (people underestimate how much strangers enjoy deep talk); Motivational Interviewing OARS (open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries — built for ambivalent people, i.e. every cold prospect); Gottman (every reply, even a decline, is a bid for connection you turn toward or away from).

## Warning finding (challenges our play)
Directional Reddit sentiment: cold calendar invites read as "presumptuous and invasive, it assumes acceptance." Community-preferred pattern: message first, invite after interest signal. Our cover-email-first structure + genuine outs + empathy protocol are the counterweight; the silent-decline rate is partly this effect. Monitor decline reasons for "presumptuous" signals. ## Observed Wins (append here; the self-learning section)
- 2026-07-20 Eliot @ Historic Films: "we already have this, built it 15 years ago" objection. Reply validated his history ("nothing to apologize for... your opinion is worth the 30 minutes") instead of countering. He replied "friday would be better" within 4 minutes. Principle: validate the identity behind the objection, don't fight the objection.
- 2026-07-20 Bill Stafford @ Raycom: invite never reached him after a real call. Cover OWNED the mistake plainly ("a calendar error on my end"). Accepted same day, 2:11 PM. Principle: admit mistakes instantly.
- 2026-07-20 Jeff Attinella @ Tampa Bay Rowdies: declined 7/31; reply offered an off-season October slot matching his real calendar pressure (in-season = no time). Accepted. Principle: move to THEIR season, demand nothing.
- 2026-07-14 TCT + CETIN: declined then re-accepted/counter-proposed within hours when the door stayed open. Principle: decline = timing, not no; never slam the door same-day.

## Non-negotiables when applying any of this (from human-messaging-SKILL.md)
No em dashes. No banned phrases. Never fabricate specifics. One real question. Room for no. Read-it-aloud test. The technique must be invisible: if the prospect can see the move, it stopped working (Tier 1, #4).
