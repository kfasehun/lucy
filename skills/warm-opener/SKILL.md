# Warm Opener (Lead With the Human, Not the Pitch)

## The shape

Warm line first, honest pivot second, business third:

> Hey Joe,
>
> Not sure if you caught the World Cup final, but that second half was the best 45 minutes of the year. Anyway, my name is {seller} and I cover {territory} at {company}. I'm reaching out because...

The pivot is honest and unceremonious ("Anyway," "On to why I'm actually writing"). Never pretend the warm line was the reason for the email. Prospects respect the transparency; they resent the bait-and-switch.

## Source hierarchy (only use tiers 1-3; tier 4 is banned)

1. **What they said publicly, professionally-adjacent**: their LinkedIn posts, conference talks, podcast appearances, company blog posts, public bios ("die-hard Bills fan" in a speaker bio is fair game).
2. **Shared moment**: a big cultural or sports event happening NOW that their city, industry, or a stated interest connects them to (their city's team in a final; a festival their company sponsors; the big industry trade show that week).
3. **Location-based ambient context**: their city's season, weather event, or civic moment. Safe because it's shared, not scraped.
4. **BANNED**: personal social media (Instagram, Facebook), family, health, home, anything they didn't publish in a professional context. If finding it would feel like surveillance to them, it is. Field evidence: stacking personal research beyond one artifact produced a "these messages are a little creepy" permanent do-not-contact.

## The five gates (all must pass or drop the warm open entirely)

1. **Verify the fact with a live search.** Never trust memory for scores, winners, dates, or names. Congratulating the wrong team ends the relationship before it starts. If it can't be verified today, cut it.
2. **The over-a-beer test.** Would a normal person say this line to a stranger at a bar and get a smile? "Did you catch the final?" passes. "I noticed you graduated from Michigan in 2011" does not.
3. **Never take a side against them.** If their allegiance is unknown, stay neutral or celebrate the quality of the moment ("what a final"), not the winner. If their allegiance IS known (tier 1 source), side WITH them or go silent.
4. **No politics, religion, tragedy, or anything contested.** Weather, sports, food, local pride, industry events only.
5. **Recency: the moment must be warm.** A final from last night, yes. A final from five weeks ago reads as a template someone forgot to update.

## Fallback rule (most important)

If no tier 1-3 subject passes all five gates: NO warm open. Go straight to the role anchor per the human-messaging skill. A researched role-anchored opener beats a forced warm one every time. Never fake it, never stretch it, never reuse the same warm subject across a batch — two prospects comparing identical "did you see the game" emails kills the whole play.

## Workflow

1. Pull the prospect's public footprint and city (the repository has the company; verify the PERSON's location, not HQ — apply the config's geographic exclusions to the person).
2. Web-search their name + company for talks, posts, bios; search their city + this week for shared moments; verify any fact found.
3. Score candidate subjects against the five gates; pick one or none.
4. Draft: warm line (1-2 sentences max), honest pivot, then the normal structure for the seller's role per the prospecting-engine skill. The warm line replaces nothing; it only precedes.
5. Log the warm subject used in the batch manifest (a "Warm subject" column) so it never repeats to the same company and the seller can mine which subjects convert.

## Voice notes

Use the seller's voice from SELLER-CONFIG.md §7. Casual is fine in the warm line, contractions always, no exclamation-point stacking, no emojis. The warm line should sound slightly imperfect, like a human typed it fast. All hard language rules from the human-messaging skill still apply. The deeper research base on warmth and rapport lives in the human-messaging skill's references (Human_Cordiality_Repo.md) — read it when calibrating.

## Examples

GOOD (tier 2, big shared moment, verified): "Not sure if you caught the World Cup final, but that second half was the best 45 minutes of the year. Anyway..."
GOOD (tier 3, location ambient): "Hope you're surviving the first real storm week of the KC summer. Anyway..."
GOOD (tier 1, they posted about it): "Your post on the acquisition made the rounds here. Building athlete-owned infrastructure is the right instinct. Anyway..."
BAD (tier 4, surveillance): "Saw your marathon photos this weekend, congrats!"
BAD (unverified fact): "Glad Spain won it" (did they? verify or cut)
BAD (stale): "That Super Bowl was something" (in July)
BAD (fake familiarity): "Hope you and the family had a great weekend!"
