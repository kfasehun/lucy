# Objection Handler — A Decline Is Timing, Not No

## Before writing anything

1. Read the prospect's actual words. Classify: timing / incumbent vendor / budget / build-in-house / process objection (they disliked the method itself, e.g. an unsolicited hold) / hard no.
2. Research 5 minutes: their website, what they actually use today (page source often shows the vendor), recent company news. Find the TRUE gap, not the imagined one.
3. Check the repository: how many touches so far? A decline after 3+ touches gets a graceful close, not a reply.

## The reply formula (one reply per decline, maximum)

1. **Acknowledge cleanly.** Their no is legitimate. No guilt, no "sorry to hear."
2. **Ask the real reason, or teach something.** Either ask which of two honest hypotheses is right ("Is this a timing thing or are you locked in with {vendor}?") or share one specific thing they likely don't know that's relevant to their stack. Give value even if the meeting never happens.
3. **Leave the door open without an ask.** "If {trigger event} ever changes that, I'm easy to find." Then stop.

Log the stated reason in the repository. Reasons are the learning loop: mine them monthly for patterns.

## Process objections (they objected to getting a calendar invite)

Do NOT apologize for the method. Explain it confidently:

- Fair reaction, here is the thinking.
- Most first meetings die on scheduling, not interest: two people trade availability for two weeks and the conversation never happens.
- The hold removes that friction. It costs one click to decline, and when they declined it disappeared, exactly as designed.
- Door stays open.

Confidence over contrition. The play is deliberate.

## Never

- Re-invite after a decline.
- Send a second objection reply if the first gets silence.
- Argue with their choice of vendor.
- Discount before a conversation has even happened.
